About Me

From Physics to Computational Neuroscience

I am a physicist working at the intersection of complex systems and computational neuroscience. My scientific journey began in physics, where I became interested in how collective behavior emerges from the interactions of many individual components.

This perspective naturally led me toward complex networks and, eventually, neural systems. Today, I develop theoretical and computational models to investigate neural dynamics, network organization, memory, oscillations, and collective behavior.

I am particularly interested in building minimal models that are simple enough to understand mathematically, yet rich enough to reproduce meaningful collective phenomena observed in biological systems.

Research

My research explores how microscopic interactions, network structure, and dynamical rules give rise to collective phenomena in complex systems.

Neural Network Dynamics

Investigating emergent activity, excitation–inhibition balance, oscillations, memory, and information processing in recurrent neural networks.

Complex Networks

Studying how network topology, modularity, connectivity, and interactions influence collective dynamics.

Transient Synaptic Memory

Developing minimal biologically grounded models in which finite synaptic activity creates a transient memory of recent network states.
